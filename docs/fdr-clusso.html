The substitution

One line, twice

Call sites A and B are the entire interface. PS‑Fdr never touches its estimator any other way — it just needs a selected set back. CLUSSO produces exactly that. This is why the instinct is right, and why the authors of both papers gestured at it.

But watch what has to be inserted inside the resampling loop.

Holds up

  • Permuting y is a valid null. The clustering step is unsupervised, so shuffling the outcome decouples precisely what it should and leaves the design construction intact.
  • Sign non‑identifiability does not bite. Only the product β⊗α is identified, but PS‑Fdr reads the support of β, and the support is identifiable.
  • No limiting distribution required. Which is fortunate, since CLUSSO has only a proven error bound, never a derived distribution.

Does not

  • The design matrix is manufactured inside the loop rather than supplied from outside it.
  • Two fits on identical data can return different selected sets.
  • The nested loops demand B+MB≈5050 fits, each potentially dragging a mixture model over 225,000 points.
  • “Fix the number selected” assumes a coefficient path that CLUSSO does not have.

Ranked by how much they hurt

Where it breaks

  • 01

    The selection frequency stops meaning one thing

    CLUSSO’s objective is bilinear in α and β, hence non‑convex. The paper concedes the algorithm reaches a stationary point rather than a global minimum, which is why it runs five random restarts and keeps the best objective. So the fitted β^ is a function of data, λ, and seed.

    That means Πj confounds two sources of variation: bootstrap resampling variability, which stability selection exists to measure, and optimizer noise, which is nothing. Meinshausen and Bühlmann’s framework assumes the selection map is fixed given the resample. Under CLUSSO it is not, and the optimizer noise appears in both the real and null arms without any guarantee it cancels.

    Where: the “non‑convex, seed‑dependent” node in the right‑hand column above.
  • 02

    The design matrix is estimated, not observed

    In the FDR paper, X is fixed data sitting outside the loop and only the resampling varies. In CLUSSO, the design matrix is the output of a clustering step — it is part of the estimator. So the bootstrap forces a choice the original paper never confronts.

    Re‑clustering each resample is honest, since it propagates clustering uncertainty into the selection frequency, and CLUSSO’s error is dominated by clustering accuracy. But it introduces label switching across bootstraps and costs enormously. Freezing the clustering is cheap and matches the fixed‑X assumption, but then the FDR control is conditional on one clustering solution and understates variability.

    Sharper still: because clustering is fitted jointly across the whole cohort, subject i’s design row depends on every other subject’s tubules. Resampling subjects is therefore not resampling independent units.

    Where: the dashed amber box that has no counterpart on the left.
  • 03

    The compute does not close

    PS‑Fdr needs B+M×B fits — about 5,050 at the paper’s defaults. With frozen clustering each fit is an alternating solve on a 2×57×250 tensor: seconds. With re‑clustering inside the loop, each one needs a Gaussian mixture over roughly 225,000 tubules in 57 dimensions: minutes.

    Which is to say the decision in obstacle 02 is not merely philosophical. It is the difference between an afternoon and something on the order of two weeks of serial compute.

    Where: the amber box, multiplied by both nested loops in PS‑Fdr step 2.
  • 04

    No path to walk

    Fixing the number of selected variables on permuted samples is PS‑Fdr’s central defence against underestimating the false discovery count. With a lasso you simply walk the coefficient path to a target size. CLUSSO has no path at all — every λ demands its own fit, with no warm-start structure to walk along. And the map from λ to support size is not even guaranteed monotone: sweeping 60 penalty values across three toy datasets, one of the three saw the selected set grow as the penalty was raised, because different λ land the alternating optimizer on different stationary points. Usually monotone, not reliably so — which is precisely the wrong property for a device that must hit an exact target size on every one of thousands of permuted samples.

    Where: the underlined constraint in PS‑Fdr step 2.

Still open

What has to be decided

Framed as “plug CLUSSO in where the lasso goes,” this is an application note, and a referee will observe that estimator‑agnosticism was already claimed. Framed as extending false discovery control to estimators whose design matrix is itself estimated by an unsupervised pre‑step, and whose objective is non‑convex, obstacles 01 and 02 stop being problems and become the contribution.

  • Inside the loop, or outside it?

    Re‑cluster every bootstrap, freeze once, or implement both and let the gap between them be the finding.

  • What to do about optimizer noise

    Fix the initialization for determinism, keep the restart convention, or measure how much of the selection frequency it actually accounts for before deciding.

  • Which regime is the target

    On the real data q=57 and CLUSSO already selects a single feature — controlling FDR on a singleton demonstrates little. The leverage is in the simulation settings where q reaches 150 to 200 and CLUSSO’s false positive rate sits between 20 and 30 percent.
